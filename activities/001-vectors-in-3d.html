Vectors in 3D

Calc 3 lives in three dimensions, and its basic unit of currency is the vector: an arrow with a length and a direction, written as three numbers. Today: how to see them, add them, stretch them, and measure them.

What you're looking at

A vector \(\mathbf u = \langle u_1, u_2, u_3\rangle\) means "go \(u_1\) in x, \(u_2\) in y, \(u_3\) in z." It has no fixed home; we just like drawing it from the origin. Drag the figure to rotate it. A single 2D screen can't show depth, so rotating is how your brain recovers the third dimension.

Adding is tip-to-tail: walk along \(\mathbf u\), then along \(\mathbf v\). Componentwise, \(\mathbf u + \mathbf v = \langle u_1+v_1,\ u_2+v_2,\ u_3+v_3\rangle\). The green diagonal of the parallelogram is the sum, whichever order you walk.

Scaling by a number \(c\) stretches the arrow by \(|c|\) and flips it if \(c<0\). It never rotates it to point somewhere new, which is exactly what "parallel" means: \(\mathbf v\) is parallel to \(\mathbf u\) when \(\mathbf v = c\,\mathbf u\) for some \(c\).

Length is Pythagoras, twice

Turn on Show how |u| is built. The dashed line runs along the floor (the xy-plane) to the point under the tip of \(\mathbf u\). Its length is \(\sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2}\) from ordinary 2D Pythagoras. Then a vertical leg of height \(u_3\) climbs to the tip, making a second right triangle:

$$|\mathbf u| = \sqrt{\left(\sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2}\right)^2 + u_3^2} = \sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2}$$

That formula also gives the distance between two points: the distance from \(P\) to \(Q\) is the length of the vector \(Q - P\).

Why this matters for the rest of Calc 3. Almost everything ahead is built from these operations. Tangent lines are \(\text{point} + t\cdot\text{direction}\). Velocity is a vector. A gradient is a vector pointing uphill. Vector fields put an arrow at every point in space. If adding and scaling arrows feels natural, the rest has something to stand on.

Things to try

  1. Press Cancel out. What is \(\mathbf u+\mathbf v\)? What does the parallelogram collapse into?
  2. Watch the triangle inequality line in the readout. Can you make \(|\mathbf u+\mathbf v| = |\mathbf u|+|\mathbf v|\) exactly? What has to be true about the two arrows?
  3. Turn on c·u and sweep \(c\) from 2 down to −2. Where is \(|c\,\mathbf u|\) smallest? What happens to the direction as \(c\) crosses 0?
  4. Find a vector of length exactly 3 with no zero components, without pressing the preset. (Hint: \(1+4+4=9\).) Can you find one of length 6?

Check yourself

1. What is the length of \(\langle 2, -3, 6\rangle\)? \(\sqrt{4+9+36} = \sqrt{49} = 7\). (Signs don't matter for length since every component gets squared.)
2. Find the unit vector (length 1) pointing the same way as \(\langle 2,-3,6\rangle\). Divide by the length: \(\tfrac17\langle 2,-3,6\rangle = \langle \tfrac27, -\tfrac37, \tfrac67\rangle\). Scaling by a positive number keeps the direction, and \(1/|\mathbf v|\) makes the length 1.
3. Are \(\langle 1, -2, 3\rangle\) and \(\langle -3, 6, -9\rangle\) parallel? Yes: \(\langle -3, 6, -9\rangle = -3\,\langle 1,-2,3\rangle\). They point in opposite directions along the same line.
4. How far apart are the points \(P=(1,0,2)\) and \(Q=(4,4,2)\)? \(Q-P = \langle 3,4,0\rangle\), length \(\sqrt{9+16+0}=5\).
